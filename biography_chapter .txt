%=============================================================
%  BACKGROUND: building – top third only + gradient fade
%
%  Strategy:
%   1. Clip to top 1/3 of page
%   2. Draw the image at reduced opacity (0.18)
%   3. Overlay a white→transparent rectangle using the
%      tikzfading so the image dissolves into white
%=============================================================
\newcommand{\AddBackground}{%
  \AddToShipoutPictureBG*{%
    \begin{tikzpicture}[remember picture, overlay]
      % ── Step 1: image clipped to top third ──────────────────
      \begin{scope}
        \clip (current page.north west)
              rectangle
              ([yshift=-0.3333\paperheight] current page.north east);

        \node[opacity=0.35, anchor=north] at (current page.north) {%
          \includegraphics[
            width=\paperwidth,
            height=0.3333\paperheight,
            keepaspectratio=false
          ]{building.jpg}%
        };
      \end{scope}

      % ── Step 2: gradient overlay (white fade from bottom of image) ──
      % Covers the bottom 60% of the image region so the fade
      % starts early and ends cleanly in white
      \fill[white, path fading=top fade, fading angle=180]
        (current page.north west)
        rectangle
        ([yshift=-0.3333\paperheight] current page.north east);

    \end{tikzpicture}%
  }%
}

\noindent orn on March 21, 1768, in Auxerre, France.
Joseph Fourier was the son of a tailor, but he would rise to prominence as one of the most
influential mathematicians and physicists of his time. Fourier lost his parents at an early age
and was subsequently raised by a Benedictine abbey, where he received his early education.
Showing exceptional promise in mathematics, he pursued his studies at the École Royale Militaire
of Auxerre.

\bigskip
\indent In 1787, Fourier began teaching mathematics at the Benedictine college in his hometown.
His academic career was briefly interrupted by the French Revolution, during which he became
involved in political activities. He was appointed as a commissioner for the National Convention,
a role in which he narrowly escaped execution during the Reign of Terror.

\bigskip
\indent After the Revolution, Fourier returned to academia and continued his work in mathematics.
He joined the newly established École Normale Supérieure in Paris in 1795, where he studied under
renowned mathematicians such as Joseph-Louis Lagrange and Pierre-Simon Laplace.

\bigskip
\indent Fourier's most significant contribution to science came during his time in Egypt with
Napoleon Bonaparte's expedition. Appointed as the secretary of the Institut d'Égypte, he conducted
important research in heat transfer. This work culminated in his seminal publication,
\textit{Théorie analytique de la chaleur} (The Analytical Theory of Heat) in 1822. In this
treatise, Fourier introduced the concept of Fourier series, a mathematical tool that allows the
decomposition of functions into sine and cosine components.
